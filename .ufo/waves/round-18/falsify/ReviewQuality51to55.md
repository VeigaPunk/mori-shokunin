## Findings

### Medium — criterion 53 conflates row binding with ledger validity

`corpus/QUALITY.md:166-170` says a non-final or duplicate started row “is unbound,” but frozen C3 separates these predicates: `bound(row)` depends on payload SHA-256, payload byte length, and task count; attempt order, timestamps, exactly-one-final-start, and started IDs determine whether the *ledger* is clean (`.ufo/waves/round-18/frozen/c3-ledger-format.json:19`). The implementation follows that split: payload identity mismatches increment `unbound` and mark a row unbound (`tools/mori-dispatch-ledger.py:234-247`); duplicate/non-final starts produce `ledger_findings` (`:284-289`), and a ledger with those findings but no unbound rows fails as `LEDGER_FINDINGS` (`:313-338`). Thus a correctly payload-bound duplicate-start row is a failed ledger, not an unbound row. Conversely, a payload byte-length or `tasks_count` mismatch is unbound under the frozen rule and implementation, but 53's examples omit both. The contract already says these arms must verify “unbound/failed” (`.ufo/waves/round-18/contract.md:39-43`); align 53 with that distinction: payload SHA/length/task-count problems are unbound, while ordering/start-cardinality violations fail ledger verification. This keeps the criterion falsifiable without making the frozen-valid implementation fail its own quality rule.
