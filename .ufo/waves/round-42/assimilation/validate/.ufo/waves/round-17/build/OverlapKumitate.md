# OverlapKumitate — C1 detection boundary

## Axes and decision

- **Coverage (axis 6; C1):** improved by adding a read-only census of terminal transcript states and counts grouped by seat prefix and provider/model, as specified in the round contract.
- **Operability (axis 7; C2):** unchanged by this C1 proposal. There is no need to couple C1 to C2 or to fleet dispatch to obtain the coverage capability.
- **Answer:** provider/model grouping is not, by itself, routing ownership. C1 stays within detection/reporting if those values remain observational labels and the tool neither recommends nor triggers operational action. It would cross the boundary if a `dead_*` class or aggregate were treated as route/capacity evidence, used to select or change a model, advance fallback, or cause retry/resume/replacement/dispatch.

## Exact detection-only boundary wording

> **Detection/report only:** `by_provider_model` groups only the `provider` and `model` values carried by the terminal transcript message; these are descriptive observations, not verified route identities, lane ownership, root cause, or capacity determinations. The scanner reports transcript classifications and seat counts only. Its output confers no routing or dispatch authority and is not a control signal or routing recommendation. The scanner does not read or write route configuration, resolve or change provider/model choices, advance fallback, retry, resume, cancel, replace, or dispatch seats. Those operational decisions remain with the existing owner of the relevant OMP or fleet surface; fleet model routing remains with the host/operator under the fleet mission. A `dead_*` result means only that the last observed terminal message matched that class; it does not prove permanent death, and counts seats rather than incidents.

This is a bounded clarification of “Detection and report only,” not a transfer of Kumitate or fleet authority to Mori. It avoids claiming all OMP operations are owned by Kumitate while making clear that the grouping itself has no control semantics.

## Risks and second-order effects

- **Runtime:** An exit status of 1 means at least one transcript matched a `dead_*` class, not that fallback is warranted. Feeding it into a runner could route on evidence weaker/different than Kumitate's typed capacity classes. [INFERENCE] Provider/model totals may invite an operator to infer a failing provider bucket even though the scan neither validates route identity nor establishes cause.
- **Contract:** The bounded tail can miss a later terminal message; substring error tokens can classify unrelated text; `unavailable`/`incomplete` do not make exit 1 under the specified mapping. The last-state and seat-count limits are therefore essential, not optional explanatory text. The existing contract also contains a garbled stdout sentence at `contract.md:60-61`; it should be resolved by the integration owner so consumers do not infer an extra status/control channel.
- **Substrate/ownership:** Fleet route selection and OMP per-seat route/fallback are distinct layers. Keep the result detached from both; if any future consumer wants to act on it, that is a separate routing-policy change with the proper owner and evidence, not an extension of C1.
- **Verification:** The C1 fixture gate can establish expected classification/output and input-hash stability, but by itself cannot prove absence of a routing integration. After implementation, verify the source reads only contract-allowed transcript fields and contains no route-config, fallback, resume, or dispatch calls; keep the gate's fixture assertions on classifications/counts and input hashes. Do not interpret its `dead_*` exit code as route eligibility.
